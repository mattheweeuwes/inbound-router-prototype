How it works

When it doesn't go cleanly

  • Refused at the door. A signed door answers 401 to a bad signature and 503 when its signing key isn't set, and stores nothing. A re-delivered event is a no-op.
  • Held for a person. The company matches no known organization (a benefits or HR contact at an unknown company is the exception: it is resolved as a low-confidence inferred employer and routed by tier), or matches two or more about equally, or only the model calls the lead a non-buyer. For spam and vendor pitch that always holds it, at any confidence, because they would otherwise be dropped unseen. For any other non-buyer call under 0.8 confidence it holds it too (at 0.8 or above the call counts, recorded as the model's). It waits in Needs a person with its candidates and the reason, and RevOps decides before any rep is alerted. If the account already has an owner, it goes to that owner instead (R5), and the alert says the model doubted it.
  • Keeps going on the rules. If the model times out (8 s), answers off-list or isn't confident, the rules decide and the reason is recorded. A registry lookup that times out (4 s) is recorded as no answer; if nothing else places the company, it goes to a person.
  • Flagged, not hidden. The rep alert goes out before the HubSpot write, so a slow or failing CRM never delays it. A HubSpot write that fails is marked on the lead's trail with its status; one that throws (a network error or the 10 s timeout) is retried once, then retried again at the next boot. A step that errors stops that lead and shows as Step failed in the activity feed.
  • Never to a rep. Disposable emails, competitors, job seekers, press, vendor pitches, students and non-US organizations go to marketing or are dropped by rule.

Decisions that shape it

  1. IDs, not names. Accounts are keyed on the AHRQ health-system ID, CMS certification number, NPI or payer, so one system is one account in the CRM.
  2. Resolve before you score. A lead the router can't place goes to a person with candidates. It guesses one thing, a probable self-funded employer, and labels it low confidence.
  3. The model classifies; code writes the facts. Alerts quote the prospect's own words and insert registry facts. Nothing generated reaches a prospect. The model can't set urgency, override a rule, write alert text or get a lead dropped on its word alone, but its use-case label can change the team for API-type leads.
  4. The model alone never drops a lead. A spam or vendor-pitch call the rules don't share always goes to a person. Any other disqualifier the rules don't share counts only at 0.8 confidence or above, recorded as the model's call; below that, a person decides.
  5. Owner first. An account that already has an owner goes to that owner, so two reps never work one account.
  6. Alert first, then CRM. The rep is told before HubSpot is written. Nothing in the alert depends on the CRM, so a slow or failing CRM never delays it.
  7. Idempotent by design. A re-delivered webhook is a no-op, a repeat contact attaches to the first lead that went to sales (a guide download that only went to nurture does not swallow the demo request after it), and repeat alerts are suppressed for 24 hours.
  8. Gated by an eval. The classifier is scored on a sealed set of 24 synthetic leads and fails below set floors, and each run records hashes of the set and the classifier. CI runs the rules-only gate on every push.

On Serif's real stack

  • HubSpotCompanies upserted on a unique account-key property; contacts by email; tasks carry the SLA.
  • CalendlySigned invitee.created webhooks, UTMs passed through to first- and last-touch fields.
  • Clay web intentAccount visits raise account intent and create a research task, not an alert.
  • PostHog / productTry Signal sessions and searches become product-qualified signals on the account.
  • Databricks + HightouchAccount scores modeled in SQL, synced to HubSpot by reverse ETL.
  • Classifier modelA slot behind one interface, filled by evidence. The demo's small model is the baseline; a larger open-weights model such as Qwen or GLM on Serif's hardware, or a hosted model under Serif's data terms (an Anthropic provider is in the code, off here), takes the slot only by beating it on Serif's own labelled leads.
  • Closed-won / lostOutcomes feed back into the weights; conversion by tier is the check that the model is right.
