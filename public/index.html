When it doesn't go cleanly

  • Refused at the door. A signed door answers 401 to a bad signature and 503 when its signing key isn't set, and stores nothing. A re-delivered event is a no-op.
  • Held for a person. The company matches no known organization (a benefits or HR contact at an unknown company is the exception: it is resolved as a low-confidence inferred employer and routed by tier), or matches two or more about equally, or, when the model ran before the route, only the model calls the lead a non-buyer. For spam and vendor pitch that always holds it, at any confidence, because they would otherwise be dropped unseen. For any other non-buyer call under 0.8 confidence it holds it too (at 0.8 or above the call counts, recorded as the model's). It waits in Needs a person with its candidates and the reason, and nothing is alerted to a rep until a person decides. If the account already has an owner, it goes to that owner instead (R5), and the alert says the model doubted it.
  • Doubted after the alert. Most leads are routed and alerted on the rules, and the model reads them afterwards as a second opinion. A disqualifier only the model raised does not un-route the lead: a review item opens and a follow-up goes to whoever was alerted and to RevOps (for an owner route and a round-robin route; a repeat touch is recorded only). The rep keeps the lead unless a person rules it not a buyer.
  • Decided by a person. In Needs a person, someone chooses the account, sends the lead to nurture, rules it not a buyer, or clears the model's doubt. The decision is stored with the lead, the lead runs through the router again with it, and every later run keeps it. A decided item cannot be decided again, and a lead still running cannot be decided.
  • Keeps going on the rules. If the model times out (8 s), answers off-list or isn't confident, the rules decide and the reason is recorded. A registry lookup that times out (4 s) is recorded as no answer; if nothing else places the company, it goes to a person.
  • Flagged, not hidden. The rep alert goes out before the HubSpot write, so a slow or failing CRM never delays it. With a live portal, each lead's writes wait in an outbox: a network error, a 429 or a 5xx is retried with backoff, honoring Retry-After, and after 8 attempts the plan is marked dead and counted on the Overview. In dry-run, the default, the requests are written to files.
  • Retried, then given up. A lead whose run throws is retried twice, after 5 s and then 10 s. After that it is given up, shown as failed, and RevOps gets an alert. A stopped process picks up unfinished leads at the next boot.
  • Never to a rep. Disposable emails, competitors, job seekers, press, vendor pitches, students and non-US organizations go to marketing or are dropped by rule.

Decisions that shape it

  1. IDs, not names. Accounts are keyed on the AHRQ health-system ID, CMS certification number, NPI or payer, so one system is one account in the CRM.
  2. Resolve before you score. A lead the router can't place goes to a person with candidates. It guesses one thing, a probable self-funded employer, and labels it low confidence.
  3. The model classifies; code writes the facts. Alerts quote the prospect's own words and insert registry facts. Nothing generated reaches a prospect. The model can't set urgency, override a rule, write alert text or get a lead dropped on its word alone, but its use-case label can change the team for API-type leads.
  4. The model waits only when it matters. The rules run on every lead. A lead waits for the model before its route only when the rules cannot settle its use case; every other lead is alerted on the rules and gets the model's second opinion afterwards.
  5. The model alone never drops a lead. A spam or vendor-pitch call the rules don't share always goes to a person. Any other disqualifier the rules don't share counts only at 0.8 confidence or above, recorded as the model's call; below that, a person decides.
  6. Owner first. An account that already has an owner goes to that owner, so two reps never work one account.
  7. Enterprise deals skip the queue. A person at an enterprise account, or at a known organization whose message has a deal-size cue, at tier B or better, goes to a named rep (R10), with 15 minutes for tier B; the first such lead of an account in 30 days also tells the enterprise deals channel.
  8. A referral is credited. A named referrer is resolved to its organization, adds intent points and leaves a signal on the referring account.
  9. Alert first, then CRM. The rep is told before HubSpot is written. Nothing in the alert depends on the CRM, so a slow or failing CRM never delays it.
  10. A list alerts once per team. An uploaded list is routed on the rules, and its queue, owner-task, nurture and review rows go out as one digest per list and team once the list is quiet. A rep alert still goes out on its own.
  11. Prepare at once, commit in lanes. Each lead's lookups start when it is accepted; what depends on earlier leads commits one lead at a time, in order within its organization's lane, so round-robin and dedupe never race and one slow lead holds only its own lane.
  12. Idempotent by design. A re-delivered webhook is a no-op, a repeat contact attaches to the first lead that went to sales (a guide download that only went to nurture does not swallow the demo request after it), and repeat alerts are suppressed for 24 hours.
  13. A person's decision is kept. A review decision and an outbound link decision are stored with who made them (the typed name: sign-in is not built) and survive every re-run and reload.
  14. One finding, two uses. The rate file becomes one finding per group, keyed on its EIN. It drives two outbound plays, and it adds a line about the prospect's own rate to an inbound brief. Code inserts every figure, a group someone already has is left alone, and nothing outbound is sent.
  15. Gated by an eval. The classifier is scored on a sealed set of 24 synthetic leads and fails below set floors, and each run records hashes of the set and the classifier. CI runs the rules-only gate on every push.

On Serif's real stack

  • HubSpotCompanies upserted on a unique account-key property; contacts by email; tasks carry the SLA. Writes go through an outbox with retries.
  • SlackRep alerts, list digests, the enterprise deals note and the doubt follow-ups as Block Kit payloads; recorded in this prototype, not sent.
  • CalendlySigned invitee.created webhooks, UTMs passed through to first- and last-touch fields.
  • Clay web intentAccount visits raise account intent and create a research task, not an alert.
  • PostHog / productTry Signal sessions and searches become product signals on the account. Feature events joined to deals drive Feature to pipeline; in this prototype the events and deals are invented.
  • Databricks + HightouchAccount scores modeled in SQL, synced to HubSpot by reverse ETL.
  • Classifier modelA slot behind one interface, filled by evidence. The demo's small model is the baseline; a larger open-weights model such as Qwen or GLM on Serif's hardware, or a hosted model under Serif's data terms (an Anthropic provider is in the code, off here), takes the slot only by beating it on Serif's own labelled leads.
  • Sign-inNot built. A person's decision records the name they type, and the routes that take decisions are served only with the demo actions on.
  • Closed-won / lostOutcomes would feed back into the weights; conversion by tier is the check that the model is right.
